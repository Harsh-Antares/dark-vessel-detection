 DARK-VESSEL DETECTION ON SAR — WHAT THIS PROJECT IS
=======================================================================

THE 30-SECOND VERSION
---------------------
Roughly 1 in 5 large commercial fishing vessels operates "dark":
physically present on the ocean but broadcasting no AIS identity signal
— and a disproportionate share of that happens inside protected waters
where fishing is illegal. Satellite radar (SAR) sees ships at night,
through clouds, and regardless of whether they are broadcasting. This
project trains a deep-learning detector on the xView3-SAR dataset
(~1,000 Sentinel-1 radar scenes, 220k+ labelled vessels), separates
dark from cooperative vessels by fusing radar detections with AIS
records, and turns the result into a map of estimated illegal-fishing
pressure inside a marine protected area (MPA).

The one-sentence framing that defines the whole project:
    "The radar sees it. AIS doesn't. That discrepancy IS the signal."


WHY IT MATTERS
--------------
Illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing is a multi-billion-
dollar problem and a leading driver of fish-stock collapse. Enforcement
leans on AIS — transponders broadcasting each ship's identity and
position — but AIS is trivially defeated: a captain who wants to fish
where they shouldn't just switches it off. Those vessels vanish from
conventional monitoring. SAR is an active sensor (it provides its own
microwave illumination), so a metal hull lights up against the dark sea
surface whether or not anyone is cooperating. The intelligence value
isn't the ship; it's the mismatch between what radar sees and what AIS
reports.


THE KEY INTELLECTUAL HONESTY POINT
----------------------------------
You CANNOT tell from a radar image whether a ship is broadcasting AIS.
A dark trawler and a cooperative trawler scatter microwaves
identically. "Dark" is therefore not a visual class the neural network
predicts — it is the RESULT OF A DATA-FUSION STEP: a radar detection
that fails to match any AIS message within a space-time window is
labelled dark. (This is exactly how xView3's own labels were made.)
The machine learning earns its keep where ML is genuinely needed:
finding tiny, low-signature vessels with high recall, rejecting fixed
infrastructure (wind turbines, platforms, buoys), classifying fishing
vs non-fishing, and estimating vessel length — because "small, dark,
fishing vessel inside an MPA" is the precise signature of illegal
fishing.


THE FIVE-STAGE PIPELINE (and where each lives in the code)
----------------------------------------------------------
Stage 1 — TILING  (src/dark_vessel/data/tiling.py, scripts/02)
  Scenes are ~29,000 x 24,000 pixels — far too big for a GPU. We read
  1024x1024 windows straight off disk (rasterio windowed I/O), with
  128 px overlap so no vessel is cut in half by a tile seam.
  SAR values are normalised (dB clip to [-35, 0] then scale to [0,1])
  and stacked with ancillary layers: bathymetry (where fishing is
  plausible) and wind speed (conditions sea clutter). The land mask
  drops useless land tiles. Crucially, every tile remembers its scene
  offset and the scene keeps its geotransform, so every detection maps
  back to a latitude/longitude.

Stage 2 — DETECTOR  (src/dark_vessel/models/model.py)
  A vessel in this imagery is a handful of bright pixels — basically a
  point, not a box. So instead of a bounding-box detector we use a
  CenterNet-style heatmap model: a U-Net (ResNet encoder + skip-
  connected decoder) outputs a per-pixel "vessel centred here?" score;
  each true vessel is a small Gaussian peak in the training target.
  Trained with a penalty-reduced focal loss to survive the extreme
  imbalance (10 vessel pixels per million ocean pixels). At inference,
  local maxima of the heatmap above a threshold are the detections.

Stage 3 — ATTRIBUTE HEADS  (same file)
  Small heads on the shared features predict, at each detection:
  vessel vs fixed-infrastructure, fishing vs non-fishing, and vessel
  length (regressed in log-metres). Trained jointly with the heatmap
  (weighted multi-task loss, losses.py). These attributes are what
  turn "a blob" into "a small dark fishing vessel".

Stage 4 — SAR x AIS FUSION  (src/dark_vessel/fusion/ais_correlation.py)
  The dark/cooperative split. In xView3 mode we inherit the AIS-
  correlation status baked into the labels (confidence HIGH = AIS-
  matched = cooperative; MEDIUM = radar-visible but unmatched = dark)
  by nearest-neighbour matching our detections to ground truth. A live
  mode is also implemented: interpolate real AIS tracks to the SAR
  acquisition time and match within a radius — no match means dark.
  There's also an optional calibrated logistic model P(dark | length,
  fishing, score) usable as a triage risk score — explicitly a prior,
  never an identification.

Stage 5 — MPA PRESSURE MAP  (src/dark_vessel/analysis/, visualization/)
  Dark detections are spatially joined to marine-protected-area
  polygons from the WDPA database. Per MPA we report counts, the
  dark/cooperative ratio, and density per km^2 per pass. Then the
  careful part: a SAR image is one instantaneous snapshot, so turning
  "k dark vessels per pass" into "estimated dark-vessel-HOURS over a
  window" is an extrapolation. We model per-pass counts as a Poisson
  process, bootstrap the rate, correct for the detector's measured
  dark-recall, and report a 95% interval with every assumption stated
  in a methods note. The deliverable is one interactive map
  (outputs/maps/pressure_map.html): MPA boundary, blue cooperative
  dots, red dark dots, a dark-density heat surface, and the hours
  estimate with its uncertainty band.


HOW SUCCESS IS MEASURED  (src/dark_vessel/evaluation/metrics.py)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Not one number — a breakdown that shows judgement:
  * Detection F1 (200 m matching radius) — the headline.
  * RECALL ON THE DARK SUBSET — the metric that actually matters;
    catching non-broadcasting vessels is the entire point.
  * Close-to-shore recall — coastal clutter is the hardest regime.
  * Vessel vs non-vessel F1 — rejecting turbines/platforms/buoys.
  * Fishing-class F1 and aggregate length error.
  * An xView3-style aggregate for leaderboard comparability.


WHAT'S IN THE REPO
------------------

                                downloads, training runs)
  LEARN_THE_CONCEPTS.txt      — the deep-dive teaching document

SKILLS THIS PROJECT DEMONSTRATES
--------------------------------
Geospatial deep learning - SAR/remote-sensing fundamentals - large-
raster engineering (tiling, windowed I/O, geo-referenced stitching) -
heatmap/center-point detection - multi-task learning - data fusion
(SAR x AIS) - extreme class imbalance - uncertainty-aware evaluation -
turning model output into a decision-ready geospatial product.
